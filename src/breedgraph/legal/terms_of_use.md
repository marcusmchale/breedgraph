## Who sees the data you submit

You control who sees what you submit. Data in BreedGraph is held by teams: when you submit data, you choose the team that holds it and how widely it is released:

- **Private**: only members of the team who have been given read access.
- **Registered**: everyone signed in to BreedGraph.
- **Public**: anyone, without signing in.

What you submit is private unless you choose otherwise. The administrators of the team that holds the data can release it more widely, or withdraw it to a narrower release, at any time. Withdrawing it stops others seeing it in BreedGraph from then on, but cannot undo what they saw or copied before, nor the licence or terms they got it under.

While data is private, the team that holds it decides who reads it, by giving them read access, and can agree with them, outside BreedGraph, the terms they use it on, for example in a collaboration or consortium agreement. Those who read a study's data through the team that holds it are not asked to accept the study's licence.

Two things are always public, as everyone needs them to describe their data: the shared ontology, the terms and variables that data is described with, so what you contribute to it is public (see "The ontology"); and the countries in which locations are placed.

We show what you submit only to the people you release it to, and to those who run BreedGraph where they need to. BreedGraph records who entered or changed data, but who did it is shown only to those who can see the record crediting that person: by default, the team that holds it (see our privacy notice).

## The ontology

The shared ontology is published under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), and each of its versions names its licence. What you add to the ontology, you license under CC BY 4.0, to be credited collectively, as the contributors to the BreedGraph ontology. If a Person record is linked to your account, you can also choose to be named among the contributors to the versions your work is in, which shows everyone your record's name, institution and ORCID iD; credit the ontology by citing BreedGraph, the version you used and the contributors it names. Some entries come from other sources under their own licences, such as the CRediT contributor roles from NISO, under CC BY 4.0: each shows its source and the credit it asks for, which you keep when you use the entry.
