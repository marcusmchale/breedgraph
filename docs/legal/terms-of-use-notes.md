# Notes for the legal office on the BreedGraph Terms of Use

The Terms of Use are a draft for the University's legal office to confirm, revised on 23 October 2026 after
comments from the Data Protection Officer, and on 29 October 2026 to set out the terms of data released beyond its
team. Not legal advice. They propose wording rather than leaving questions open;
these notes list what to confirm. Text in [square brackets] is a fact to fill in
before publishing.

Changes in this revision, after comments from the Data Protection Officer:

- Only what owners release to everyone is public; the Terms no longer suggest that all germplasm and locations are.
- "Who sees the data you submit": users choose how widely what they submit is released, and it is private to their
  team unless they choose otherwise.
- "What you add": personal data about another person, such as a record crediting them, only with their consent.
- "How we use what you submit": a permission limited to running BreedGraph.

Changes in the revision of 29 October 2026, on the terms of data released beyond its team:

- "Study licences": a study, and its datasets, can be released beyond the team that holds them only once the study
  has a licence, which covers the files they cite too. BreedGraph offers CC BY 4.0 and CC0 1.0, and teams can give their own. "Data without a licence may be used
  for research" is gone: study data released beyond its team always has a licence.
- "Other information released beyond its team": what has no licence of its own, such as germplasm, locations, units,
  field layouts and files, is under CC BY 4.0 when released to everyone, and for signed-in users' own research, not to
  be published or passed on outside BreedGraph, when released to signed-in users only. The terms cover the
  information, not plant material or rights in it, and never personal data.
- "The ontology": published under CC BY 4.0. Contributors license what they add under CC BY 4.0 and are credited
  collectively, as no one's name is shown on pages open to everyone. Entries from other sources, such as the CRediT
  roles, keep their own licence and credit.
- "Who sees the data you submit": while data is private, the team that holds it agrees its own terms with those it
  gives read access to, outside BreedGraph.
- "Credit": users cite the attribution BreedGraph shows with the information, the teams that hold it. A team sets its
  attribution, which is public, and teams within it are credited by it unless they set their own; a team that sets
  none isn't named. This is how CC BY 4.0's "identification of the creator" is met for what teams release.
- "The ontology": contributors are still credited collectively, and those who choose to be are also named, with each
  version their work is in. Crediting a version means citing it and the contributors it names.

To confirm for that revision:

1. **Releasing licenses.** That a team's administrator, releasing the team's information to everyone, can license it
   under CC BY 4.0 for the team, and that the Terms can say so.
2. **Database rights.** That the University licenses its own rights in BreedGraph's information as a whole, such as
   database rights, on the same terms ("Other information released beyond its team").
3. **Collective credit.** That crediting ontology contributors collectively, naming only those who choose to be
   named, is reasonable attribution under CC BY 4.0.

To agree with the University's intellectual property team: any further use the University may make of data submitted
to BreedGraph, and its terms ("How we use what you submit").

Before publishing, the facts in square brackets are filled in, including the limit of the University's liability.
