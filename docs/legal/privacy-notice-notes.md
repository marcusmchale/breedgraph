# Notes for the DPO on the BreedGraph privacy notice

The privacy notice is a draft for the Data Protection Officer to confirm, revised on 23 October 2026 after the
DPO's comments. Not legal advice. It proposes answers rather than leaving them open; these notes list what to
confirm. Text in [square brackets] is a fact to fill in before publishing.

Changes in this revision, after the DPO's comments:

- Consent is now the lawful basis for most personal data: users consent when they register, and Person records are
  made only with the consent of the person credited. Public task remains for what running BreedGraph securely needs.
- Those who submit data decide how widely it is released, and Person records are private to the team that made them
  unless released, with the person's agreement.
- Who entered or changed data is recorded, but who did it is shown only to those who can see their Person record
  ("Who entered or changed data").

Since then (28 October 2026), place searches on the map no longer go to a third party: our server answers them from
its own copy of GeoNames' list of places, so the OpenStreetMap Foundation (United Kingdom), whose Nominatim service
received each search until now, receives none, and no transfer to the UK is made for them. "Maps and place search"
says so. Searches are not logged.

Also since then, the emails telling admins that a request or offer awaits them (to link a Person record, for access
to a team, a transfer of control) no longer name who asked or offered, nor a record's number: they say what awaits
and where, and the admins see who on the site, where they may. A copy in a mailbox is beyond the reach of an account's
deletion or a Person's erasure. An invitation still names who invites, and a contact message its sender, whose name
is their point.

Also since then (29 October 2026), the Terms of Use set out the terms of data released beyond its team: study
data under its study's licence, other information released to everyone under CC BY 4.0, and the ontology under CC BY
4.0. "Research data" now says that released data can be reused by others, which is why it should hold no personal
data, and that licences and the Terms never cover personal data. "Who entered or changed data" says that ontology
contributions are credited to the contributors as a whole, not by name.

Also on 29 October 2026, teams can set an attribution, the text they are credited by, which is shown to everyone with
what they release, so that the Terms' credit can name them. It is the only thing about a team shown to everyone, and
only when the team sets it. The summary and "Research data" say so, and that it should name an organisation or group,
not a person without their agreement.

Points to confirm:

1. **Lawful basis.** Consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) for account data and Person records; public task (Art. 6(1)(e)) for server
   logs, failed sign-in counts, backups and the erasure log.
2. **Records.** Whether BreedGraph needs a data protection impact assessment, or an entry in the University's record
   of processing activities, and what you need from us for either.
3. **Transfers outside the EEA.** Which safeguard covers what partner users outside the EEA see (Art. 13(1)(f)).

Before publishing, the operator fills in the contact address (a role address, rather than an individual's), the DPO's
details, the safeguard for transfers outside the EEA, and the date.
